### Brief History of Trello

Trello is a tool founded and released in 2014 by Joel Spolsky, a Fog Creek founder. It raised about $10.3 million in funding from Spark Capital and Index Ventures.

By 2016, Trello had over 1.1 million daily active users and over 14 million signups. This was quite impressive, considering the level of competition around that time and the duration it took them to hit that milestone.

Trello was then acquired by Atlassian for about $425 million. In 2018, Trello acquired another company called Butler which added some power-up features for automating your tasks in the Trello dashboard.

Over the years, Trello has undergone upgrades to become what it is today, a very popular project management tool for individuals and businesses.

#### Keyboard Shortcuts

Trello is equipped with a functionality that allows people to use keyboard shortcuts to make their work easier. This will not only save you time wasted by clicking on every item you need, but it makes handling tasks even easier.

Some of the notable keyboard shortcuts features you can take advantage of include the following:

*   B – To access the Board Menu.
*   N – To add a new card to any list.
*   Q – To view your cards only.
*   F – To access the filter function to see the assigned Trello cards.
*   D – To make a due date when viewing a card.
*   C – To archive a card and clear space.
*   SPACE – To assign yourself a card.
*   ? – To open up more shortcuts options.

### The Pros and Cons of Trello

**Advantages**

**Disadvantages**

  
*   Trello is equipped with well-organized card and board systems that make work easier and reduce confusion since everything can be assigned to a specific color and order for easier tracking.

  
*   It relies heavily on system data despite being cloud-based software. If you’re cut off from the internet, you will not be able to use it.

*   Smooth editing capabilities that help users to keep the projects well organized and easy to edit when the need arises. This is also available on the Trello free plan.

*   Trello is one of the project management tools with limited storage capabilities. The free version only allows people to attach up to 10 MB of data. This can be expanded to 250 MB on Trello premium, which is still limited by all standards.

*   The ability to collaborate through the many features makes it easy for tasks, notes, and assignments to be shared in any format between your team members. You can also share the tasks directly via Google Drive or DropBox.

*   It is not ideal for large teams. It is convenient for small teams and individuals, but once the team grows and expands, things start getting complicated, and errors start popping up every time and then.

*   You can prioritize tasks with flexible deadlines that work with your schedule. This high level of flexibility makes Trello a good option for time-constrained people.

*   The commenting feature needs a lot of improvements. Users are only allowed to comment but no editing. Once a comment has been sent, the only way to edit it is to write a new one from scratch.

*   It has a searchable database that helps users quickly find anything they need when the data is too much.
