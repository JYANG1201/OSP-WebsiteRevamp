Simply put, [](https://onesearchpro.my/how-to-drive-traffic-to-your-website/)**[organic traffic](https://onesearchpro.my/how-to-drive-traffic-to-your-website/)** is the number of visitors that land on your site from search engine results pages without any paid advertising. Free exposure—pretty neat, huh?

Now, you might wonder how this free traffic banquet relates to SEO. It’s simple: the better you optimise your site for search engines, the more potential customers can find you. Imagine tossing a frisbee; good SEO is like a strong, accurate throw, bringing more people to your website picnic.

Here’s an example for you. If your website is all about healthy eating, and you’re ranked high for search terms like “best low-carb recipes,” more health-conscious foodies are likely to find you. As a result, you’ll enjoy more organic visitors hungry for your content.

So, what brings these virtual window-shoppers to your site organically? A few sources to consider are:

*   **Search Engines:** Your main source, where SEO efforts shine.
*   **External Links:** Other websites find your content worthy and link to it.
*   **Social Media:** While not direct, engaging posts can lead followers to check you out.

Just remember, like a well-watered garden, organic traffic doesn’t grow overnight. 

It takes time, patience, and often a witty sense of humor, like mine, to keep things in perspective as you nurture your website’s presence on the web.

### Difference Between Organic Traffic and Paid Traffic

When you’re looking to boost your website’s visitor count, you’ll hear about two main types: **organic traffic** and **paid traffic**. 

Imagine organic traffic like a garden; it grows over time when you’ve nurtured it with quality content and smart SEO strategies. 

It’s the crowd that finds you through search engine results, not because you’ve waved a flashy sign (aka an ad) at them, but because your site has what they’re seeking.

On the flip side, paid traffic is like a vending machine — you put money in and get visibility out, almost instantly. 

This is the traffic you receive from ads; you pay for the clicks you get, whether that’s through Google Ads, social media ads, or other paid channels.

## **Metric 2: Keyword Ranking**

Keyword ranking might sound like a term tossed around by tech wizards, but actually, it’s about knowing where you stand in Google search results. 

Imagine you run a race and you want to know your speed, right? Keyword ranking is like your personal leaderboard for specific words or phrases people search for.

Tracking where your website lands on search results can be as thrilling as a mystery novel. 

If you’re climbing up the ranks, kudos! 

Your SEO strategy is on point. But if you’re slipping down, it’s a nudge to revisit your game plan. Think of it as a compass that points to whether your SEO efforts are sailing smoothly or if you’re veering off course.

If you see an improved ranking, it often means more eyes on your site and a better chance of attracting customers. 

In contrast, a decline in ranking could signal it’s time to pump new life into your content or reassess your keywords. 

After all, the internet is dynamic and not everything is set in stone.

Your strategy should always aim to place your website in the VIP section of search results—the first page! 

This way, you’re more likely to draw the crowd—the clicks and the customers—right to your stage.

## **Metric 3: Backlinks**

Imagine [](https://onesearchpro.my/seo-backlinks/)**[backlinks](https://onesearchpro.my/seo-backlinks/)** as votes of confidence from other websites, pointing to yours saying, “These folks know their stuff!” 

But like all votes, some count more than others.

That old saying about quality being better than quantity? Spot on for backlinks. You might have a bunch of links, but if they’re from shady sites, they won’t do you any favours. Search engines give more weight to backlinks from trusted, authoritative websites.

Think of your links as friends. 

You’d want friends who get you, who are into the same stuff, right? The same goes for backlinks. 

Links from websites that are relevant to your industry or topic can help boost your site’s credibility. For instance, if you’re selling sports gear, a link from a fitness blog can be pure gold.

## **Metric 4: Indexability**

![SEO Health | One Search Pro Digital Marketing](https://lh7-us.googleusercontent.com/docsz/AD_4nXdQ7Vzu6lgs1cOFcdxP7ojlwJJgJLzfoQCmEMAjXpgi_0hnwWMHuqtjny0PnpIsMXVNlMuxex6r_24_no0vKvChRGCOSSVNsIjJH7-0w8-jm-qPXwtCij3j9ZgjScBiz4WgcMzCJMTMRLvsCMMcFul1fSw?key=Z92hYVogFu6zrNDHNq3CTg)

_Using Google Search Console, you can see an overview as to why some of your website pages are not indexed. Image source:_ **_Break the Web_**

Indexability is your website’s invitation to the search engine party. It refers to the ability of a search engine, like Google, to analyse and add your pages to its vast library of web pages.

Essentially, Google is a digital librarian. If your book (website) isn’t indexed, it’s like it’s not even on the shelf for people to find. Poor indexability means you’re pretty much invisible to Google’s searching gaze.

Hence, it’s wise to monitor your website’s indexability. By doing so, you’ll spot any indexing errors quickly. An unnoticed error can prevent your page from being indexed entirely. 

This is like throwing a party but forgetting to send out the invites – _no one will show up!_

In an unfortunate scenario where your pages get deindexed, it’s a stealthy issue that could hurt your website’s visibility. Without visibility, your website might as well be stranded on a deserted island. 

Short story – keep your pages accessible for Google’s crawlers. If they’re not being indexed or have been deindexed, act fast to fix it, or else your online presence could suffer.

## **Of Myths and Truths: Metrics That Don’t Matter That Much**

The blessing of being thorough can actually a double-edged sword. While we admire the dedication in taking every metric into consideration, there are certain metrics that don’t really affect your SEO health drastically.

So, remember not to worry too much if these numbers aren’t looking spectacular. They do not necessarily reflect the overall health of your website’s SEO.

Here are the metrics we’re referring to:

### Bounce Rates

![SEO Health | One Search Pro Digital Marketing](https://lh7-us.googleusercontent.com/docsz/AD_4nXelJdyPWk-PnoAXx7CFDFdCITJV1-ZFyHo50V8vR5VQ7yOBA3Ysv5WKOHlWAcn55MuPFRZRX3lvgsHDzjOYBbJT9Z2o9kfWXy0Utz3UTGME814Zb1bwzGq_kQQKneD48B27gUAkb2s04uwdq9Hm53q_N0nX?key=Z92hYVogFu6zrNDHNq3CTg)

_On Google Analytics, you will be able to see your website’s bounce rate as shown above._

To break it down: bounce rate is simply the ratio of single-page visits to total visits. But here’s the twist—high bounce rates are often viewed as a party foul, indicating that the website might not be as engaging as needed.

However, bounce rate is more like a misunderstood hero of SEO metrics. It’s not always about the content not vibing with the audience. 

Sometimes, visitors find exactly what they need on the first page they land on, so they have no reason to click around. They got the golden nugget they were looking for and left satisfied.

When you’re looking at bounce rates for your site, remember, that you can’t judge a book—or a website—by its cover. 

You need to segment your data to understand the story behind the numbers. Some pages might have a higher bounce rate because they’re the end of the line for the visitor’s journey.

Informational pages, for example, serve up answers with no further action needed.

So, before you rush to revamp your site’s design or content over a high bounce rate, stop and consider the context. 

What was the visitor’s intent? What did they do right before bouncing? It’s about finding the “why” behind the “bye.”

### Exit Rates

Think of exit rates as a buzzer that sounds every time someone leaves your website from a specific page. You might hear the buzz and worry that something’s wrong with the page. But, before you jump to fix it, let’s break down what an exit rate really tells us.

First off, exit rate measures the number of people who leave your site from a particular page compared to the total number of views the page has. 

So, if your blog post on “The Best Chocolate Chip Cookie Recipes” has 100 views and 10 people take off after reading it, your exit rate for that page is 10%.

Now, feel the relief wash over you because exit rates aren’t necessarily the red flags they’re often seen as. 

Let’s say your readers find exactly what they need on your cookie recipe page, bake some delicious cookies, and then close the tab contentedly. Your exit rate there simply signifies a job well done!

In essence, exit rates give you hints, not verdicts. They show where visitors are stepping out, but it’s your role to figure out why. 

Sometimes it just means your content hit the mark. Other times, it might nudge you towards making some tweaks.
